A FUNCTIONAL APPROACH TO REMEDIES
This book will take a functional approach, looking at the dominant goals of 
different remedies. The general goal will be identified, and individual rem -
edies that share that goal will be discussed. We divide remedies according to 
the following general functions:
 1. compensation;
 2. compulsion;
 3. deterrence;
 4. vindication (including punishment);34 and
 5. restitution.
Rafal Zakrzewski has criticised functional approaches, suggesting that the use 
of ‘compulsion’ or similar as a goal should not be included in a series includ -
ing ‘compensation’, ‘punishment’ and ‘restitution’, because it is taxonomically 
incoherent.35 He suggests instead that a distinction should be made between 
‘replicative remedies’ (which replicate primary rights or secondary rights) and 
‘transformative remedies’ (which create a new legal relationship different to 
that which existed before trial). Ying Khai Liew has suggested that a further 
category of ‘reflective remedies’ should be added to Zakrzewski’s taxonomy 
(these, like replicative remedies, give effect to secondary rights, but are more 
discretionary).36
Under this analysis, payment of a debt or specific performance is a replica -
tive remedy as is an award of compensatory damages for tort, for example. 37 
A remedial constructive trust is a transformative remedy which creates a new 
right altogether,38 but as discussed in Chapter 11, English law does not recog-
nise such trusts. Under Liew’s system, the sliding scale of remedial response 
to equitable estoppel (which can range from specific performance to equitable 
compensation) would be a reflective remedy.39

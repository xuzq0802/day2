WHAT IS A REMEDY?
Colloquially, we might think of a remedy as a ‘cure’ or a way of ameliorating a 
wrong.17 However, it is necessary to be more specific when discussing the legal 
nature of remedies. Lawyers have used the term ‘remedy’ in a variety of dif -
ferent ways, including to describe a cause of action, a substantive right, a court 
order, a means of enforcing a court order, and a final outcome of litigation.18
Another way of conceptualising legal remedies is to see them as a response 
to civil wrongdoing.19 According to this view, a remedy confers a ‘right’ on 
the claimant because the claimant then has the ability to enforce a correla -
tive ‘duty’. Hence, the scholar John Austin argued that remedies are ‘second -
ary rights’, which only arise when there is a violation of a ‘primary right’

If the plaintiff has a right, he must of necessity have a means to vindicate and main-
tain it, and a remedy if he is injured in the exercise or enjoyment of it; and, indeed, 
it is a vain thing to imagine a right without a remedy; for want of right and want of 
remedy are reciprocal. 59
However, it has been said that it is unclear what is meant by ‘vindication’. 60 
Jason Varuhas has argued for a more specific sense of vindication, where the 
law attests to, affirms, and reinforces the importance and inherent value of 
particular interests. 61 He argues that vindication is particularly evident in the 
torts actionable per se (ie, without the need to prove loss), which protect inter-
ests such as bodily integrity,62 property rights,63 reputation,64 economic inter-
ests65 and fair process.66
The categorisation of ‘reasonable fee’ awards had been contested. Now that 
the United Kingdom Supreme Court has decided in One Step Support Pty Ltd 
v Morris-Garner that ‘reasonable fee’ awards of various kinds operate to vin-
dicate private law rights,67 these are included in Chapter 8.
